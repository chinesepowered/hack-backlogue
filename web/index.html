Privacy

Nobody's backlog is accurate

You're three minutes into a review. Something looks good. To save it, every other app asks you to leave the video, open it, search for the game, and file it.

Almost nobody does that. That's why almost nobody's backlog is accurate.

Backlogue lives in your share sheet instead. Share the video, the thread, or the store page into it and the game is in your pile in one tap — without leaving what you were watching.

It remembers where you found it

"Hollow Knight: Silksong — from a YouTube video."

One extra line under every game. Six months later your list reads like a record of your own taste instead of a chore list.

Built not to make you feel bad

A backlog is guilt made visible for most players. Every "pile of shame" joke is someone telling you their tracker made them feel worse. So this one is designed against its own genre.

No completion percentage

No progress rings, no "47 unplayed games" counter, no red badges, no overdue states. Those are the mechanics that turn a collection into an obligation.

"Bounced", not "Abandoned"

A game you gave four hours and didn't click with is Bounced. It puts the mismatch on the game rather than on you.

Art-forward and dark

Cover art is the most beautiful thing on the screen and we didn't draw it, so the interface gets out of its way.

Alerts that earn it

A nudge when a game you wanted finally gets a release date, or the day it lands. Never a reminder that you haven't played something.

Free for the part that matters

Saving, organising, rating and sharing up to 30 games is free and always will be. A paywall in the middle of saving a game would destroy the two seconds the whole app exists to protect.

Backlogue Pro lifts the cap and adds the release alerts. That's it.

Backlogue is built with Kotlin Multiplatform and runs on Android and desktop. Source on GitHub.
